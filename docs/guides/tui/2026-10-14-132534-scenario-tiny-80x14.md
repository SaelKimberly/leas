## Purpose and scope

Pins the behaviour below 20 rows: the app starts *compact* (a one-line status bar instead of the
8-row panel), the table's viewport can be smaller than the loaded page, and `F1` flips to the
full-panel overlay.

Does not cover: a resize *during* the run (all sizes are launched fresh), colour, or the mouse.

- No other expectation was wrong: the clipping at 80x14 (E2/E3), the `F1` overlay (E4) and the return

## Tooling friction

Same harness limits as `profiles-import-and-table` (pyte wide-char crash patched in the runner; wide
chars collapse one cell; buffer reads are text-only; no liveness check). Additional ones:

- **At this size the "screen" is 14 rows and a capture is the only way to see the whole layout.** Row
  arithmetic is unforgiving: an off-by-one prediction reads an empty cell (as in `narrow-80x24`), so
  the full-screen `capture` at steps 3, 7, 9 and 11 is the load-bearing evidence here.
- **`F1` is in the special-key vocabulary but nothing documents it as a state change** — the harness
  sent it fine (`✓ Sent 1 special key(s)`), but the resulting overlay is only observable through a
  capture, and the app gives no on-screen confirmation that the mode changed.
- **The compact bar's content is timing-dependent** (it mirrors the newest log line), so it cannot be
  asserted with `expect` in a scenario that does not also control the log; E1's correction records the
  two readings observed 4 steps apart.

## Review of findings

Below 20 rows the app makes a good call — keep the table, collapse the panel to one line — and then
lets `F1` undo it in the worst possible way. That, plus the invisible clip at 14 rows, is what this
scenario adds.

**1. `F1` at a small size replaces the working screen with the log panel (major).** Evidence: step 9 —
the content area is entirely ` ⚙ Actions Log  ////…`, `○ Disconnected`, `🖥 …`, `⏱ …`, `📊 …`, `📋 Core:
…` and no table, while the tab bar still says `Profiles`. Why it matters: the toggle is advertised as
`[F1] Actions Log`, i.e. a panel toggle; at 14 rows it becomes a mode switch that hides the app's main
view, with no title, no hint and no obvious way back (F1 again — which the hint row does not mention
either). Recommendation: never let the panel take the content area; overlay it, or cap it and scroll,
and put `[F1] Close panel` in the hint row while it is up.

**2. The table clips a row without saying so (minor).** Evidence: step 3 — 6 data rows drawn, footer
` Server: 203.0.113.77:443  [xray]  rows 1-7 of 7`. Why it matters: `End` proves the row is reachable
(step 7), but nothing on the initial screen hints that endpoint 7 exists; the footer even asserts
`1-7`. Recommendation: make the footer state the visible range (`rows 1-6 of 7`) or size the thumb by
the viewport.

**3. The compact bar clips its message (minor).** Evidence: step 4 — `| startup: version=0.1.0 page=7
rows tcp-timeout`. Why it matters: the one line of information the compact mode offers ends mid-token,
which reads as a rendering fault. Recommendation: ellipsize; at these widths prefer the selection
summary alone.

**4. Inconsistent connection wording between the two panel forms (nit).** Evidence: step 4's
`○ vless 203.0.113.77:443 [xray] | …` against the full panel's `○ Disconnected` (doc `narrow-80x24`
step 7). Why it matters: the same state reads as a glyph + protocol at one size and a word at another.
Recommendation: one wording, both sizes.

### Verified good

- The `<20`-row decision is right and immediate: a fresh 80x14 launch starts with the table visible and
  a one-line actions bar — `○ vless 203.0.113.77:443 [xray] | …` (step 3/4) — not the 8-row panel.
- The table window follows the selection: `End` scrolls the viewport so the last endpoint is visible
  and the footer/breadcrumb track it (` Server: us-nyc-04.synthetic-node.example:8388  [xray]  rows 1-7
  of 7`, step 7) — the selection is never off-screen.
- `F1` is a clean toggle: the same keystroke returns the exact previous window and the compact bar
  updates to the current log line (step 11).
- The compact bar carries the useful minimum — selection protocol/address and the newest log line — in
  one 80-column row, and it keeps the connection glyph (step 4).
